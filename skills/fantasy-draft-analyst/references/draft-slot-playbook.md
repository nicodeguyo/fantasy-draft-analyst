# Draft-slot playbook — what your slot actually gives you

Your draft slot decides two things: which tier you get first, and how far apart your picks are. Everything else is a consequence. This reference gives the shape of each slot type; the simulator gives the specific names for a specific year.

## 2. The three slot types

**Early (top third).** You get a tier-1 player — usually the best available bell-cow RB or the top receiver — and then wait almost two full rounds. Your second and third picks are paired at the 2/3 turn but they come after 20+ players are gone. Strength: the one player who can win weeks alone. Weakness: the long gap means you must draft the position that's about to run out at the turn, not the best player.

**Middle.** Evenly spaced picks, one per round, roughly T picks apart. You never get a paired turn, so you can't plan combinations — but you're never stuck waiting either. Strength: flexibility, best-player-available drafting works. Weakness: every pick is a coin flip on who survives from the previous pick, and players sitting between your picks (ADP within ~T/2 of your pick) are the ones you have to decide on now or lose.

**Late (bottom third).** Paired picks at every turn (14/15, 42/43, 70/71…). You miss tier 1 entirely but choose two players from the next tier at once. Strength: combinations — RB + RB, or elite TE + RB, planned in advance. Weakness: the 27-pick wait between turns in a 14-team league means whole tiers vanish between your picks; you are always drafting the end of a tier, never the start.

## 3. Early slot, turn by turn (12-team shape)

- **R1**: the best player on your surplus board. If a true tier-1 RB is there, take him; the RB replacement gap is the reason. If the board starts WR-heavy, the elite receiver is fine — but then your R2/R3 turn must be RB + RB.
- **R2/R3 turn (24/25)**: this decides your season. Twenty-three players are gone. Take the last two players of the tier that is about to end — almost always two starting backs, or a back plus the last elite TE.
- **R4/R5 turn (48/49)**: the WR band is deep here; two receivers with locked route shares.
- **R6/R7 (72/73)**: best available at RB/WR, plus your first look at the QB tier if the scoring is QB-friendly.
- **R8+**: QB (if not taken), TE (if not taken), then upside.

## 4. Middle slot, turn by turn (12-team shape, slot 5–8)

- **R1**: tier-1 if available; otherwise the top of tier 2 at the scarcer position.
- **R2 (~17–20)**: the best remaining bell-cow. The middle-slot R2 pick is where keeper inflation bites hardest — expect an ADP-25-to-30 player here in a keeper league.
- **R3 (~29–32)**: RB again unless an elite TE or a tier-2 WR clearly out-surpluses the backs. This is your only realistic elite-TE window in most years.
- **R4 (~41–44)**: the reach window. If a role-certain RB sits in the dead zone at a committee price, this is where you take him — one round early, not two.
- **R5–R6 (~53–68)**: receivers. The band is flat and deep; take the highest route share.
- **R7 (~77)**: QB window in 4-point-TD leagues. Don't let it slide two more rounds in leagues with 14 teams.
- **R8 (~92)**: TE if you waited, or your second QB-tier look.
- **R9+**: handcuffs, second-year upside, K/DEF last.

## 5. Late slot, turn by turn (12-team shape, slot 11–12)

- **R1/R2 turn (12/13)**: two from the top of tier 2. The classic is RB + RB; the alternative is elite WR + elite RB. Do not take a QB here in a 1QB league whatever the platform's default rankings say.
- **R3/R4 turn (36/37)**: last starting-caliber RB plus the best receiver, or the elite TE plus a back if the TE cliff is steep this year.
- **R5/R6 turn (60/61)**: two receivers from the flat band, or receiver + dead-zone RB with a certain role.
- **R7/R8 turn (84/85)**: QB + TE, or QB + upside RB if the TE tier-2 is still there next turn.
- **R9+**: same as everyone — handcuffs and next-year keepers.

The late slot's edge: you see 20+ picks between turns, so by the R3/R4 turn you know exactly which tiers are gone and can plan the pair precisely. Use the "There %" at your *next* turn to decide which of two targets to take now: take the one who won't be there.

## 6. How keepers change each slot

With K keepers removed (K ≈ teams in a one-keeper league):

- If keepers cost picks, inflation is front-loaded: roughly +K×0.9 at the first pick, +K×0.6 at the R2/R3 turn, +K×0.3 by round 4, and near zero from round 6 on, because every forfeited pick puts a player back on the board. Pick 20 in a 12-keeper league delivers about the ADP-28 player; pick 77 delivers the ADP-77 player. The simulator measures the actual figure at each of your picks.
- If keepers are free (no pick forfeited), inflation is ≈K at every pick.
- Early slots lose the most: the tier-1 player may be kept already, and the R2/R3 turn is now ~30 players deep.
- Late slots gain relatively: their paired turns were already deep; inflation shifts everyone equally, but late slots planned for it.
- If the league publishes its keeper list before the draft, use it: remove exactly those players and forfeited picks. Availability estimates get much sharper.

## 7. The universal rules

1. The first three picks must be weekly starters with locked roles. Upside is for round 6 and later.
2. Draft the position that is about to run out, not the best player in a vacuum — unless the surplus gap says otherwise.
3. Between two targets, take the one with the lower There % at your next pick.
4. Don't reach for anyone whose There % at your next pick is above ~80%.
5. QB and TE have windows, not rounds: take them when the tier is about to end, and never in between tiers.
6. Every pick after round 9 is either a handcuff, a second-year upside play, or a next-year keeper. Kicker and defense in the last two rounds.
