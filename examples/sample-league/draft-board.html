This is a prepared ordering, not a forecast of your actual season. The score sums season projections for one best legal starting lineup. Weekly substitutions, byes, new injuries, waivers, and bench insurance are outside that score. The board benchmark follows the displayed choices and documented fallback; the adaptive simulation policy is a separate comparison.

Appendix

Every number on this sheet, where it came from, and what it means.

Terms

Pick value
A matched-control estimate of projected points for one best legal starting lineup after taking this candidate and simulating the remaining draft. Compare within one pick with the stated model limitations.
Gap
The modeled projected-lineup difference versus the prepared target at this pick. TAKE identifies that target. A small difference is not a proven real-season edge.
Pre-draft availability
The fraction of all simulated drafts in which a player remains undrafted immediately before a pick. Your own seat also drafts from ADP in this pass. This is not conditional survival after deliberately passing on an available player.
Monte Carlo noise
Variation from a finite number of simulated continuations with fixed inputs. The standard error does not include uncertainty in projections or the opponent model.
Surplus / vs. free
Projection minus estimated replacement at a position. It describes depth and contributes to shortlisting and fallback choices; replacement does not guarantee an equivalent player on waivers.
Cost of waiting
The modeled drop in best available positional surplus between your picks. The replacement constant cancels directly, but changed draft paths can still move the result.

Explore the whole draft, then challenge the assumptions

The pick tables compare projected starting lineups after simulated draft continuations. Replacement-based surplus still helps describe positional depth and influences candidate selection and later picks. Changing those assumptions can change the plan.

A small estimated gap means no clear model preference, not proof that two players are equivalent. Candidate availability populations can differ; a precise simulation is only as useful as its projections and opponent assumptions.

How the numbers were built

  • Saved player inputs were assembled September 3, 2026. Projections are analyst/model-authored from earlier production and role assumptions, not an official ESPN projection feed or independently validated consensus. The source record is in RUNLOG.md; this launch review did not independently reverify its player-news claims.
  • The top 100 ADPs use the saved ESPN column of the Footballguys cross-platform table; deeper names retain FantasyFootballCalculator half-PPR mock ADP and spread. Platform coverage is incomplete. New league plans need fresh, dated inputs.
  • Candidate continuations use shared prefix states and matched unforced controls. Each player is evaluated only in states where available. Different candidate populations and the later drafting heuristic can affect rankings, even with many simulations.
  • The keeper scenario pass compares a shortlist of eligible single keepers and keeping nobody before building the plan. Known keepers occupy their teams’ rosters and consume their specified round. The sample models unknown opponent keepers.
  • Availability is an unconditional pre-draft frequency. Picks marked on this HTML board update tracking only; the simulations and advice remain the saved pre-draft plan.
  • Benchmark evidence must be generated from the same model and displayed policy. Its score is the sum of season projections for one best legal starting lineup, not weekly fantasy results or measured performance against a commercial draft assistant.

Assumptions worth knowing about

  • Sample League is fictional: 12 teams, ESPN half-PPR, pick 5, one QB, two RB, two WR, one TE, one RB/WR/TE flex, K, DEF, and six bench spots.
  • One keeper per team costs the original draft round; waiver pickups are not eligible. Confirm real rules and explicit keeper ownership before using this setup.
  • Season projections may incorporate expected-game assumptions, but the simulator does not model weekly injuries, replacements, byes, waivers, or the value of bench insurance.
  • Opponent drafts follow noisy ADP and positional rules. They are not a calibrated replica of ESPN autopick or known behavior of real leaguemates.
  • The saved player inputs are for reproducing this example. Refresh source dates, roles, injury status, and scoring for your own league.
The one assumption that could flip this whole board: Challenge the player projections, opponent pricing, and later drafting policy that drive the choices. Rerun plausible alternatives when a recommendation depends on one assumption. Low Monte Carlo noise does not establish projection accuracy.
Also worth remembering: Actual draft rooms can diverge from this opponent model in either direction. Descriptive leaguemate preferences are research context unless encoded in supported inputs and rerun. Prepare backups and use the current board and your roster when making a live choice.
