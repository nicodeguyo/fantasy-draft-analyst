Why this build

Across the simulated drafts that follow this plan the starting lineup ran 1,763 to 1,877, mean 1,839 (sd 19.2); the ten sample drafts spanned 1,814 to 1,867. Read the plan as an ordering, not as a total: the same simulated drafter choosing freshly at every pick scores 1,863, so the plan trails the tool's own default policy by 24 points, about a point and a half a week. Part of that is the price of having a plan at all — the board recovers most of it by telling you to take the top row still on the board — and part is a real bias in the engine, which charges a forced pick for the rigidity of the policy that has to repair the roster around him. If every target lands the nine starters sum to 1,871. The known weakness is RB2: Jaylen Warren is a timeshare and everything behind him is 27 points below replacement, and the hedge is Breece Hall at 44 when he falls (34%), which pushes the quarterback to 53 and the tight end to 77.

Late-round plan

Brandon Aubrey is the single defensible break from 'kicker last': 155 projected against a replacement kicker's 130, 56% available at pick 140 and 2% at 164, and the pick he costs is a bench lottery ticket worth nothing to your lineup. That is about 19 points, a little over one a week. If he is gone at 140, take Cameron Dicker (68%) or Jake Bates (85%) rather than reaching back at 149 (23%). Waiver pickups cannot be kept here, so 149–173 is where you manufacture next year's cheap keeper: Jordyn Tyson is 99% at 164 and 96% at 173, and Travis Hunter is 100% at every one of your picks — the draft ends before ADP reaches him. Woody Marks (66% at 149), Zach Charbonnet (53%) and Mike Washington Jr. (56%) are the back-end depth; Kyle Monangai (31% at 116) and Rachaad White (71% at 125) are the bench backs with a path to volume. The room starts on kickers and defenses in round 11, so Seattle, Denver and Houston are all under 5% by 140; Baltimore is 86% at 164 and 79% at 173, Buffalo 99% and 98%. Claim Samaje Perine off waivers in Week 1 — he is Brown's backup, he is not in the draft pool, and he is the one handcuff that matters to this roster.

Appendix

Every number on this sheet, where it came from, and what it means.

Terms

Pick value
The projected final starting lineup you finish with if you take this player at this pick and draft sensibly afterwards, averaged over 200 simulated completions of the draft. Every pick table on this sheet is sorted by it, and it needs no assumption about what a player is 'worth'.
Control / vs. control
The same simulated drafts played out with nobody forced in at that pick, and the difference the candidate makes on his own samples. Pick values are measured against that arm, which is why they compare within a pick and never across picks.
Now
Points of final starting lineup behind the best choice at that pick. 'best' appears on the top row and on any row inside the noise band with it — a ≈ mark means the simulation cannot tell them apart.
Next
How often the player is still on the board at your following pick, across 1,500 simulated drafts in which your own seat drafts off ADP — so the number is about the room, not about your plan. Amber under 50%: take him now or lose him.
Level (≈)
Two candidates within about two standard errors of each other. Break the tie on scarcity, role certainty or who is less likely to last — never on the decimal.
Replacement level / vs. free
The score of the worst player at a position who still has to be in someone's starting lineup every week, by flex equilibrium: dedicated slots first, then every flex slot league-wide plus three bye slots to the best remaining player regardless of position. RB 164 (RB28), WR 156 (WR35), TE 116 (TE13), QB 312 (QB12). 'vs. free' is projection minus that number. It is the tier boards' column and it ranks nothing.
Flex fill
How the flex slots split by position at equilibrium: 4 RB / 11 WR / 0 TE here. The receiver pool is the deeper one, which is why the marginal back and the marginal receiver land eight points apart.
Cost of waiting
Points of value lost at a position by waiting until your next turn. The same replacement level sits in both terms and cancels, so this table does not move when the baseline does — unlike a surplus heatmap.
Keeper inflation
With twelve keepers removed and twelve picks forfeited, the player available at a pick is worse than ADP suggests. Measured here: +11 picks at 5, +8 at 20, +6 at 29, +4 at 44, +3 at 53, half a pick at 77, nothing from 92 on.

Replacement level — the explanation, not the decision

Replacement level164 RB28 156 WR35 116 TE13 312 QB12

What replacement level tells you here, and what it no longer decides

In this 12-team league with one flex, the 28th running back (164 points) and the 35th receiver (156) are the worst players anyone has to start every week — computed by giving each of the twelve flex slots and three bye slots to the best remaining player regardless of position. Those flex slots fill 4 RB / 11 WR / 0 TE, which is the clearest one-glance statement that the receiver pool is the deeper one: the marginal back and the marginal receiver land eight points apart. That is the vs. free column on the tier boards.

It is not what ranks your picks any more. Move the running-back baseline three ranks — well inside honest disagreement, since RB28 projects 164 and RB31 projects 148 — and every back on the board gains or loses sixteen points of surplus at once. The pick tables above never move, because a final starting lineup is a fact about a roster and involves no baseline at all. The clearest example on this board: Brock Bowers is +76 vs. free and Drake London +71, and at pick 20 they are level — three tenths of a point apart — because tier-2 tight end is 85% available at pick 53.

How the numbers were built

  • Projections are mine, built Sept 3, 2026 from 2025 production and 2026 roles, scored in this league's exact settings (0.5 PPR, 4-pt pass TD, −2 INT). Five were changed after draft-week research: Egbuka 206→192 (toe, no team drills as of Sept 2), Tuten 200→176 (Coen: 'more by committee', Aug 26), Irving 201→188 (Gainwell and Tucker have real roles), Nabers 207→197 (ACL, Week 1 uncommitted), Jeanty 199→191 (ankle).
  • Pick values: for each of your picks, up to eight candidates plus every player on your roster and your list were planted at that pick and the rest of the draft simulated 200 times, then the best legal starting lineup scored. Common random numbers across candidates, a matched no-forced-pick control arm on the same drafts, and a two-standard-error noise threshold. The rollouts run through round 9 (pick 101); the tables at 116 and 125 fall back to vs. free plus availability, because no pick values were computed that deep.
  • Availability: 1,500 drafts with twelve keepers drawn (weighted toward better players, keeper rounds correlated to quality), opponents on ESPN ADP plus noise scaled to each player's spread and capped, positional need from round 8, kickers and defenses on a rising curve from round 11. In this pass your own seat drafts off ADP like everyone else, on purpose — 'will he still be there' has to be a fact about the room.
  • The keeper verdict: 1,000 full drafts per scenario. Keeping Chase Brown projects 1,864; Daniels 1,827, Bowers 1,824, McMillan 1,813, keeping nobody 1,810. Three different scales live on this sheet and none of them subtract: pick values are measured against that pick's own control arm and compare only within a pick; the plan total is 1,839; the keeper scenarios come from their own free-running pass.
  • ADP: ESPN column of the Footballguys cross-platform table for the top 100 (fetched Sept 3, 2026); FantasyFootballCalculator half-PPR 12-team mocks Aug 28–Sep 2 for the spread and for players beyond the top 100. ESPN rooms take quarterbacks and tight ends a round early and let receivers slide: Metcalf +25 picks, Pierce +22, Jameson Williams +17, Flowers +11 against the mock market.
  • Betting lines: VegasInsider win totals (BetMGM, Sept 2, 2026); season props from Sharp Football Analysis (Aug 31) and Yahoo (Aug 24). Used as a check on medians, never as projections.
  • News and usage: FantasyPros and RotoWire feeds, FantasyFootballCalculator player-news pages, PFF, Bleacher Nation, Pewter Report and team sites — all fetched Sept 3, 2026, items dated June 9 to Sept 3.
  • The honest limitation: following the plan simulates to 1,839, while the same drafter choosing freshly at every pick scores 1,863 — the tool's recommendation trails its own default policy by 24 points, about a point and a half a week. Part of that is unavoidable (a plan is a pre-draft ranking; the adaptive board, 'take the top row still on the board', recovers most of it) and part is a real defect: the rest-of-draft policy inside a rollout is the same heuristic, so a forced pick leaves that heuristic repairing a roster shape it did not plan, and the candidate is charged for that rigidity as well as his own merits. It is the top item for v2.1. The rankings and the within-pick gaps survive it; the totals do not.

Assumptions worth knowing about

  • Starting lineup assumed: 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 1 RB/WR/TE flex, K, DEF; six bench. No superflex — a superflex slot would make Jayden Daniels the keeper conversation instead of Chase Brown.
  • Keeper rule assumed: one keeper at the original draft round, waiver pickups not keepable, Brown at round 6 (pick 68 forfeited). Confirm on the commissioner's sheet before Sunday.
  • Injuries are baked into expected games rather than modelled separately. Re-check Flowers (lower body), Egbuka (toe), Nabers, Jeanty, Tuten, Nacua, Chase, Hall and Henderson on Saturday's report.
  • Kickers and defenses are treated as near-interchangeable and drafted last, with one priced exception: Brandon Aubrey at pick 140 is worth 19 points over the round-14 kickers and costs a bench lottery ticket.
  • Fifteen rounds means anyone with an ADP past about 155 goes undrafted — which is why Jordyn Tyson and Travis Hunter are effectively free at 164 and 173.
  • ESPN pricing covers the top 100 only; players 101 and deeper carry mock ADP, so availability past pick 100 is approximate.
The one assumption that could flip this whole board: The projections themselves. Measuring lineups instead of replacement levels took the old flip-assumption (the RB-versus-WR baseline) off the table; what is left is the three numbers this plan leans on hardest. Jaxon Smith-Njigba 268 — if he is really a 240-point receiver, pick 5 is Bijan-or-nothing. Jayden Daniels 347 — if the rushing floor regresses, the quarterback slides to 53 and pick 44 becomes Breece Hall. Kyle Pitts 150 — if Atlanta starts Tua and Pitts is a 125-point tight end, the tight end had to come at pick 20 and this build is wrong by about 25 points. Disagree with those three before Sunday, not during.
Also worth remembering: The simulated opponents draft rationally off ESPN ADP. Your actual leaguemates don't — two of them take a quarterback by round 4 every year — so Daniels at 81% at pick 44 is optimistic and Hurts at 82% is your realistic floor. The compensation is that receivers fall further than the model predicts: when a tier-3 receiver is somehow there two rounds late, take him.
