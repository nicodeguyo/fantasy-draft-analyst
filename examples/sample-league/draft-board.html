The only number you need to remember: replacement level

In a 12-team league with one flex, the 28th running back (164 points) and the 35th receiver (156) are the worst players anyone has to start every week — computed by giving each of the 12 flex slots and 3 bye slots to the best remaining player regardless of position. Those flex slots fill 4 RB / 11 WR / 0 TE, because the receiver pool is deeper. A player's value is his projection minus that number: the Surplus column.

So draft the biggest surplus, not the biggest projection. Kyren Williams (203) out-projects Tetairoa McMillan (200) and McMillan is the better pick by five; Zay Flowers (215) is +59 while Breece Hall (203) is +39. The tight ends are the exception: Bowers and McBride sit 25 points above the next tier, and ESPN rooms take both in round 2.

Appendix

Every number on this sheet, where it came from, and what it means.

Terms

Replacement level
The score of the worst player at a position who still has to be in someone's starting lineup every week, computed by flex equilibrium: dedicated slots first, then every flex slot league-wide to the best remaining player regardless of position, plus three bye slots. RB 164 (RB28), WR 156 (WR35), TE 116 (TE13), QB 312 (QB12) here.
Flex fill
How the league's flex slots split by position once everyone starts their best player. 4 RB / 11 WR / 0 TE here — the receiver pool is deeper, which is why the marginal back and the marginal receiver land within eight points of each other.
Surplus
Projection minus replacement level. The points a player adds over the player you could have had for free. Every table on this sheet is sorted by it.
Position plan
The expected best surplus still available at each position at each of your picks — the roadmap heatmap. It answers 'when do I take which position': the outlined cell is the position with the most value left; grey means nothing above replacement.
There %
How often the player was still undrafted at that pick across 1,500 simulated drafts. Below 50%: plan for him being gone. Above 80%: don't reach.
Tier / cliff
A tier is a group of players close enough in projection that which one you get barely matters. A cliff is the point drop between tiers — pay before it or wait until after it.
Keeper inflation
With twelve keepers removed, the player available at a pick is worse than ADP suggests. Measured here: +11 picks at 5, +8 at 20, +6 at 29, +4 at 44, +3 at 53, and under one from 77 on, because twelve picks are forfeited too.

How the numbers were built

  • Projections are mine, built Sept 3, 2026 from 2025 production and 2026 roles, scored in this league's exact settings (0.5 PPR, 4-pt pass TD, −2 INT). Five were changed after research: Egbuka 206→192 (toe, no team drills as of Sept 2), Tuten 200→176 (Coen: 'by committee', Aug 26), Irving 201→188 (Gainwell/Tucker roles), Nabers 207→197 (ACL, Week 1 uncommitted), Jeanty 199→191 (ankle).
  • Replacement level by flex equilibrium: the 12 dedicated slots at each position are filled first, then the 12 flex slots and 3 bye/injury slots go to the best remaining RB/WR/TE regardless of position. That fills the flex 4 RB / 11 WR / 0 TE and prices RB28 = 164, WR35 = 156, TE13 = 116, QB12 = 312. A fixed 55/40 split would have priced RB34 = 137 and made every depth back look like a starter.
  • ADP: ESPN column of the Footballguys cross-platform table for the top 100 (fetched Sept 3, 2026); FantasyFootballCalculator half-PPR 12-team mocks Aug 28–Sep 2 for the spread and for players beyond the top 100. ESPN rooms take QBs and TEs a round early and let receivers slide 10–17 picks; that is priced in.
  • Simulation: 1,500 drafts (seed 1000) with twelve keepers drawn (weighted toward better players, keeper rounds correlated to quality), opponents drafting ESPN ADP plus noise scaled to each player's spread and capped, positional need from round 8, kickers and defenses on a rising curve from round 11; the simulated 'me' weighs the opportunity cost of waiting at each position and keeps the bench balanced. The position plan is the expected best surplus by position at each of your picks over those runs. Net keeper inflation measured in-sim: +11.2 picks at 5, +7.9 at 20, +6.1 at 29, +3.9 at 44, +2.9 at 53, +0.5 at 77, +0.1 at 92. The mock-ADP comparison uses the same simulator on players_mock-adp.csv.
  • Betting lines: VegasInsider win totals (BetMGM, Sept 2, 2026); season props from Sharp Football Analysis (Aug 31) and Yahoo (Aug 24). Used as a check on medians, never as projections.
  • News and usage: FantasyPros and RotoWire feeds, FantasyFootballCalculator player-news pages, PFF, Bleacher Nation, Pewter Report, team sites — all fetched Sept 3, 2026, items dated June 9–Sept 3.

Assumptions worth knowing about

  • Starting lineup assumed: 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 1 RB/WR/TE flex, K, DEF; six bench. No superflex.
  • Keeper rule assumed: one keeper at the original draft round (same round), waiver pickups not keepable, Brown at round 6. Confirm on the commissioner's sheet before Sunday.
  • Injuries are baked into expected games, not modeled separately. Re-check Flowers, Egbuka, Nabers, Jeanty, Tuten, Nacua, Chase, Hall and Henderson on Saturday's report.
  • Kickers and defenses are treated as near-interchangeable and drafted in the last two rounds; the room starts on them in round 11 and the top three defenses are gone by pick 140.
  • Fifteen rounds means anyone with an ADP past ~155 goes undrafted; late lottery tickets are effectively free.
The one assumption that could flip this whole board: How your league fills its flex. The equilibrium model assumes managers flex the better player (4 backs / 11 receivers league-wide), which prices RB replacement at 164 and makes receivers the value at 5, 29 and 44. If your leaguemates reflexively flex backs, RB replacement drops toward 137, Breece Hall at 44 becomes the pick over McMillan, and Warren is worth taking at 53. Count last year's Week 10 lineups: fewer than 30 running backs started means this board is right.
Also worth remembering: The simulated opponents draft rationally off ESPN ADP. Your actual leaguemates don't — two of them take a QB by round 4 every year — so QBs will go earlier than this sheet says and receivers will fall further. When a tier-3 receiver is somehow there two rounds late, take him.
